# babyloop — Channel Playbook

Strategy for a faceless, made-for-kids YouTube channel built from procedurally rendered canvas loops, procedural music-box/lullaby arrangements of public-domain melodies, and offline-TTS narration of public-domain stories. Owner: Joshua (solo, Texas). Nothing is scraped or re-uploaded; competitor research is metadata only. Figures were pulled from YouTube channel pages on 2026-09-19 unless marked otherwise. "Unverified" means no primary source confirmed it.

## 2. Why those formats work

**0–3 months: high contrast.** Newborns focus at roughly 20–30 cm, have very poor contrast sensitivity, and look longer at high-contrast patterns because bold light/dark edges are the only signal their retina and visual cortex resolve well (Fantz 1963, via Lovevery). Colour vision needs cone maturation; infants are broadly trichromatic by 2–3 months. Red is commonly described as the first hue reliably discriminated (general pediatric guidance, not a single study). Hey Bear's oldest uploads (2012–2016) are exactly this: black/white/red patterns with classical music.

**3–12 months: saturated colour, faces, beat-synced motion.** Sussex Baby Lab eye-tracking (Franklin & Newman, 3–11-month-olds, 100 picture books) found that by 3 months infants look most at bold, bright, high-contrast colour images and least at black-and-white or pastel ones. Large patches of colour and clearly defined shapes won. Faces with eye contact and predictable rhythmic motion are the strongest attention anchors.

**12–36 months: Ms Rachel's mechanics.** Elizabeth Coombes (University of South Wales, The Conversation, 17 Sep 2025) credits Ms Rachel's results to infant-directed speech ("parentese": higher pitch, exaggerated contour, animated face and gestures), simple repetitive songs with predictable openings and closings, deliberate pauses that leave room for the child to answer, prompts to copy gestures, and expressive eye contact. The University of Washington trial (Ferjan Ramírez, Lytle & Kuhl, PNAS 2020, n=71) found parents coached in parentese had children with roughly 100-word vocabularies at 18 months versus 60 in controls. Wait time matters because the goal is the child's response, not the screen's.

**The honest counterweight.** The AAP (Media and Young Minds, 2016): under 18 months, avoid screen media other than video-chat; 18–24 months, only high-quality programming watched together; 2–5 years, up to 1 hour/day, co-viewed. Its newer Q&A guidance drops strict hour counts in favour of content quality and co-viewing. Two studies drive the overstimulation critique: Zimmerman, Christakis & Meltzoff (2007) found each daily hour of baby DVDs was associated with 6–8 fewer understood words in 8–16-month-olds; Lillard & Peterson (Pediatrics 2011, n=60) found nine minutes of a show cutting every ~11 s impaired 4-year-olds' executive function versus one cutting every ~34 s. Parent-facing reviews of Hey Bear (Screenwise, nanny-agency blogs) repeat the same points: faster segments cause a "screen-time hangover," it is "digital pacification, not education," watch for fussiness and trouble settling.

**Positioning.** babyloop is the calm option: scene holds of 20–40 s, no cut faster than 10 s, no strobing, 60–100 BPM for sensory and 50–70 for sleep, moderate loudness, narration written for a co-viewing adult ("Can you point to the red apple with your grown-up?"), and descriptions that say the channel is built for short, shared sessions.

## 3. What YouTube will and won't pay for

**Made for kids / COPPA.** After the FTC's $170M settlement (Sep 2019), every upload must be set as made for kids when children are the primary audience; YouTube overrides only for error or abuse. Made-for-kids content loses: personalized ads, comments, notification bell, cards and end screens, branding watermark, autoplay on home, miniplayer, save-to-playlist / watch-later, live chat, Super Chat/Stickers, memberships, merch shelf, donate button; a channel-level setting also removes Posts. Contextual ads only, so RPM is low. Reported 2025–26 ranges: Kidscreen (Oct 2025) quotes TeamFalco's co-founder that a strong kids channel earns about US$1 RPM or lower and that kids creators lost ~50% of earnings in Jan 2020; Gyre (Jun 2026) $1–3 RPM versus $5–15 general; LearningRevolution (Mar 2026) $0.50–2; Pastory (Feb 2026) $0.25–0.35 CPM, about $350 per million views. Plan on $0.30–1.00 RPM.

**Quality principles (support.google.com/youtube/answer/10774223).** High quality: looking after yourself and others; learning and inspiring curiosity; creativity, play and imagination; life skills; showing the world's diversity. Low quality: heavily promotional; encouraging negative behaviour; deceptively educational; hard to follow (including mass-produced or autogenerated); sensational or misleading (keyword stuffing); strange use of children's characters. Since Nov 2021 channels heavily featuring low-quality kids content are suspended from YPP and removed from YouTube Kids.

**Inauthentic content (15 Jul 2025).** YouTube renamed "repetitious content" to "inauthentic content" and said it would better identify mass-produced or repetitive uploads: nearly identical narrated stories, slideshows with repeated narration, templated AI output. A loop pipeline is exactly this pattern if abused.

**DO / DON'T**

- DO set made-for-kids at channel level; DO lead descriptions with the educational hook and age range.
- DO give every upload a distinct hook (new concept, story, melody set or scene family), narration plus captions, original characters. Log the diff between uploads.
- DO cap long-form at 2–3 per week; DON'T mass-upload near-identical hour mixes or run sibling channels with the same renders.
- DO name the actual content in titles ("Colours and Counting 1–10"); DON'T stack "for babies for toddlers brain development sensory" or promise developmental outcomes.
- DON'T use anyone else's characters, names or music, including "Dancing Fruit" phrasing (Hey Bear Sensory® and Mindful Moon® are registered marks per their channel description).
- DON'T put products, unboxing or merch in the video.
- DO disclose in one line that narration is a synthetic voice and music is procedurally arranged from public-domain melodies.

## 4. Content architecture

**Loop model.** Render each scene as a 20–40 s beat-locked loop at the episode's BPM (loop length = bars × 4 × 60/BPM). Repeat a loop for 2–4 minutes, then change scene, palette or character; 30-, 60- and 120-minute cuts reuse the same loop library with different orderings. Hey Bear openly does this: "EXTENDED", "Best of" and "1 Hour Mix" titles recombine existing segments, and their 27-minute Disco Fruit Party credits six tracks, about 4.5 minutes per segment.

**Three tracks.**
- (a) SENSORY: newborn black/white/red patterns (20–30 s holds), dancing fruits with faces (beat-synced bounce, saturated primaries), bubbles (slow drift for visual tracking).
- (b) LEARN: colours, shapes, numbers 1–10, animal sounds, first words; parentese pacing, a 2–3 s pause after every question, three repetitions per concept, on-screen word captions.
- (c) STORY & SLEEP: narrated public-domain tales (Jack and the Beanstalk, Three Little Pigs, Goldilocks), Mother Goose rhymes, lullaby-star scenes at 50–70 BPM with palettes darkening over the hour.

**Cadence.** Two long-form uploads per week (one SENSORY or LEARN, one STORY & SLEEP), three Shorts cut from them, one 60–120-minute compilation per month once eight distinct loops exist. Quality beats volume: Hey Bear's 91 videos average 35.8M views; Baby Sensory Bedtime's 1,320 average 0.73M; Baby Einstein's 878 average 1.1M. Little Hues, a 2024 "dancing fruit" imitator, reached 254M views on 42 videos: proof of demand, and exactly the derivative position (trademark and inauthentic-content exposure) to avoid.

Title templates (patterns, not copies):
1. `{Brand} – {Theme} Dance Party! – Baby Sensory Video with {Music Style} Music`
2. `High Contrast Baby Sensory – {Pattern Theme} – Black, White & Red (0–3 Months)`
3. `{Brand} – Colours & Counting 1 to 10 – Dancing {Fruit/Shapes} for Babies`
4. `Sleepy {Character} Lullaby – {N} Minutes of Calm – Bedtime Video for Babies`
5. `{Story Title} – Bedtime Story for Toddlers – Read Aloud with Pictures`
6. `Animal Sounds for Toddlers – {Setting} – Learn First Words with {Brand}`
7. `{Brand} – {Season/Holiday} Celebration! – Calm Sensory Dance Video`
8. `Bubbles! – Slow Visual Tracking for 3–6 Months – Calm Baby Sensory`
9. `Mother Goose – {N} Classic Nursery Rhymes – Sing-Along for Toddlers`
10. `{Brand} – 1 Hour Mix – Best of {Series} – Baby Sensory Music Video`

Thumbnails: one big character filling 40–60% of the frame; three colours max; no text under 60 px at 1280×720 (it must read at 168 px wide); face with direct eye contact; consistent corner mark; test on light and dark UI.

Description template: two-line summary with age range and "watch together" note; chapters every 2–4 minutes at scene changes (`00:00 Red circles`, `02:30 Bubbles`); three "what your child practises" bullets; melodies with first-publication year and "original procedural arrangement"; synthetic-voice disclosure; playlist link.

Tags (12–15): baby sensory, high contrast baby video, calm baby video, bedtime video for babies, lullaby for babies, toddler learning video, colours for toddlers, counting 1 to 10, nursery rhymes, bedtime story for toddlers, black white red newborn, plus brand and series names.

Playlists: by track (Sensory / Learn / Story & Sleep), by age (0–3 mo, 3–12 mo, 1–3 yr), by length (30/60/120). They matter because viewers cannot save to playlists; you order the queue for them. End screens and cards are unavailable, so the final 20 s is an in-video "next up" card and the description's first line links the playlist.

Shorts: cut 15–45 s from the best-retaining loop, re-render at 9:16 (do not crop), set made for kids, three per week. Shorts do not appear in the YouTube Kids app (Qustodio). vidIQ reports Shorts revenue sharing still applies to made-for-kids channels; YouTube's Shorts policy page does not mention made-for-kids either way, so treat that as unverified.

## 6. Music & rights

Procedural arrangements of public-domain melodies leave no third-party recording to claim; only the composition can be challenged, and a PD composition cannot legally be a Content ID reference.

Public domain (composition): Twinkle Twinkle Little Star (melody "Ah! vous dirai-je, maman" 1761; Taylor lyrics 1806), Brahms's Wiegenlied (1868), Rock-a-bye Baby (1765/1805), Hush Little Baby (traditional), Mary Had a Little Lamb (1830 poem, Mason melody 1868), Baa Baa Black Sheep (1744), Bingo (1780), Row Row Row Your Boat (1852), Hickory Dickory Dock, Humpty Dumpty, Frère Jacques, London Bridge, Alphabet Song (1835), Old MacDonald (printed 1917; specific recordings are not), Itsy Bitsy Spider (printed 1910), Lavender's Blue, Golden Slumbers (Dekker 1603; the Beatles arrangement is not), Happy Birthday to You (ruled uncopyrighted 2015; $14M settlement and final judgment 2016), Satie Gymnopédies, Debussy Clair de lune, Bach, Mozart.

Not public domain or too risky: The Wheels on the Bus (Verna Hills, "The Bus", American Childhood, Dec 1937, often cited as 1939; status disputed, no definitive renewal record found, treat as protected); If You're Happy and You Know It (Jonico Music registered it in 1971 crediting Joe Raposo; origins contested); Baby Shark (Pinkfong's 2015–16 arrangement and recording are protected even though the camp chant is traditional; Korea's Supreme Court in Aug 2025 ruled only that Pinkfong did not infringe Johnny Only); You Are My Sunshine (1940); Puff the Magic Dragon (1963); The Hokey Pokey (1940s); Baby Beluga (1980); anything from Sesame Street, Raffi, CoComelon, Bluey; any modern arrangement or recording of a PD tune.

Content ID false claims on PD melodies happen (TunePocket documents Twinkle claims from Sony, Warner Chappell and UMPG publishing). Procedure: YouTube Studio → Content → Claims → Dispute; state the melody is public domain, cite first publication (Library of Congress or IMSLP), state the audio is your own procedural render. The claimant has 30 days; if reinstated, appeal (7-day claimant window when the video is blocked). Keep a provenance file per melody: MIDI source, render log, first-publication citation.

TTS voices: the original rhasspy/piper engine is MIT (archived Oct 2025); the maintained OHF-Voice/piper1-gpl is GPL-3.0, which governs the engine, not generated audio. Voice models carry their own MODEL_CARD licences and vary widely: en_US lessac is under the Blizzard 2013 licence (non-commercial), and many voices (amy, joe, kusal, arctic, alan, jenny_dioco, vctk, libritts_r, hfc_female) are fine-tuned from lessac; whether fine-tunes inherit that restriction is argued in piper discussion #271 and unresolved. ryan and hfc_female/male are CC BY-NC-SA, l2arctic CC BY-NC: not for a monetized channel. Trained from scratch on public-domain data: en_US kristin, norman, ljspeech, bryce, and en_GB cori (LibriVox). Use those and store each MODEL_CARD next to the render.

## 7. Launch plan (30 days)

**Week 1 — setup.** Pick a name and check YouTube, .com, USPTO and social handles the same day (availability unverified): Bloomloop, Hushpetal, Pip & Pear (pipandpear), Tinyorbit, Dozydot, Moonpebble, Littlelumen, Slowstar. Create the Google account with 2FA, set channel audience to made for kids, save upload defaults (description template, tags, English captions), banner and avatar with the main character, three playlists. Render six seed videos: (1) newborn black/white/red patterns, 20 min; (2) fruit dance party, 12 min; (3) bubbles slow-tracking, 20 min; (4) colours & counting 1–10 with narration, 10 min; (5) five Mother Goose rhymes, 10 min; (6) sleepy-stars lullaby, 60 min. Publish two on day 5, two on day 7.

**Weeks 2–4 — cadence.** Two long-form per week (alternate tracks), three Shorts per week from the best-retaining 30 s, one compilation at day 28. Every upload must differ in concept, scene set and narration from the last.

**KPIs.** Average view duration and percentage viewed (target ≥25% on 10–15 min episodes, ≥8 min absolute on 60-min sleep videos); returning viewers as a share of total; impressions CTR (2–5% is a working heuristic, not a verified benchmark); traffic mix (Browse and Suggested should dominate; Search is the launch lever). Retire any loop whose relative retention drops below 60% in its first 30 s.

**YPP math for a made-for-kids channel.** Threshold: 1,000 subscribers plus 4,000 public watch hours in 12 months, or 10M public Shorts views in 90 days. Watch hours are the easy half: 4,000 h = 240,000 min; at an 8-min average view that is 30,000 long-form views. Subscribers are the hard half: notifications and comments are off and viewers are often in the Kids app (no subscribe button) or on a parent's phone. Observed sub-to-view ratios in this niche run 0.04–0.23% (Hey Bear and Ms Rachel both 0.12%), so 1,000 subs implies roughly 430K–2.5M views. Budget 6–12 months to YPP; the 500-sub fan-funding tier is irrelevant because Super Thanks and memberships are disabled on made-for-kids content.
