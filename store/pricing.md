# Pricing and positioning — one page

**Why sell direct at all.** A made-for-kids channel under 2026 enforcement earns roughly nothing
for months (PLAYBOOK §3: plan on US$0.30–1.00 RPM, and that only once a channel is in YPP).
One personalised video at US$4.99 nets more than 15,000 made-for-kids views. The channel is the
free sample; the shop is the business.

**Position.** Not "cheap AI video". The pitch is *calm, drawn, and made for one child*: hand-drawn
cast, public-domain melodies, no ads, no autoplay, no data, delivered within 24 hours. The price
sits with a personalised board book or a custom birthday banner, not with a US$3 Canva template.

Why these numbers: the strategists' range was US$12–19 for personalised video. US$4.99 is the
price point where Etsy buyers stop comparing to templates and start comparing to gifts;
US$6.99 for the goodnight video is justified by the length and the included MP3. Odd prices
(US$14.99) look like discount retail on Etsy's kids-gift pages; whole dollars look like a
maker. The Calm Library's strike-through anchors the launch price; raise to US$29 after the
first 100 sales and to the US$29 list once there are 12+ episodes.

## Unit economics

payment processing 3 % + US$0.25, listing/renewal US$0.20 per sale (auto-renew on a
made-to-order listing renews on every sale). Offsite Ads add 15 % (12 % above US$10k/yr) only
on orders Etsy's ads brought in; budget ~20 % of orders at that rate.

Payhip's paid plans (US$29/mo → 2 %, US$99/mo → 0 %) only beat the free plan above about
US$970/month in Payhip sales; stay on free until then.

**Bandcamp**: 15 % + processor (≈ 4–5 % on small amounts). US$6 album → ≈ US$4.60 net. US$1
track → ≈ US$0.70. Treat it as discovery and provenance, not income.

**Shopify** (existing store, trial plan): Basic is US$29/mo + 2.9 % + US$0.30 online. It only
makes sense once direct sales pass ~US$800/month, i.e. after Etsy has proven demand. Until
then, Payhip is the mirror; the `shopify` key in `LINKS` stays empty.

## Time economics (the real cost)

A personalised order costs about six minutes of attention: read the box, decide the
pronunciation, run the render, listen to the name in the finished MP4, upload, paste the
delivery message. At US$13 net that is ~US$130/hour of hands-on time; the render itself runs
unattended. Ten orders a day is an hour's work. The queue, not the price, is the constraint,
so: batch renders morning and evening, and never promise faster than 24 h even when it is
usually 2 h.

## Refunds and re-renders

Budget 3 % refunds and 8 % re-renders (almost all pronunciation). A re-render costs six
minutes; a refund costs the fees (Etsy refunds the transaction fee but not the processing
fee). Offering a free re-render first, cheerfully, keeps refunds near zero and produces
five-star reviews from people whose first render was wrong.

## Launch discount

Email-list subscribers get **20 % off for the first 14 days** of each new product (Etsy: a
shop coupon code, e.g. `CALM20`; Payhip: a coupon). At US$4.99 that is US$12, still inside the
strategists' range and comfortably above cost. Don't discount the Calm Library twice: the
strike-through *is* the launch discount.

## What we don't claim

No developmental benefits, no "screen-time-free", no earnings claims in the creator licence.
Every listing discloses the synthetic voice, the hand-drawn art and the public-domain music
in plain words. That is both Etsy's rule and the brand.
